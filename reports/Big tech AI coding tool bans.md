# Big tech rarely bans outside coding tools outright

**No, not as a general rule.** As of late November 2026, a full "in-house only" ban on Claude Code and OpenAI Codex is the exception among large technology companies. The usual pattern is narrower: the company limits the outside *tool* but lets the outside *model* run inside its own tool. In the US, Google is the only one known to block both tools on its own code, and since September 2026 even Google lets every engineer choose Anthropic's Claude model inside its internal tool. Amazon restricted both tools in late 2025 and approved them company-wide in May 2026. Microsoft went the other way: it promoted Claude Code in early 2026, then moved to cancel most licences in one large group. Meta and Apple allow outside tools or models, with limits on tasks and budgets. China tightened in the second half of 2026: Alibaba banned Claude Code from 10 July, and ByteDance shut outside AI out of its most sensitive code and documents in November. For Tencent, Baidu, Huawei and Xiaomi no written rule was found, which is not the same as "no ban". Many limits also come from outside the company: Anthropic refuses to serve majority Chinese-owned companies anywhere, China's regulator warned against Claude Code in July 2026, and the US government twice restricted the use of Anthropic's models in 2026. Companies with no competing product mostly buy these tools and then limit the spending. One caution: almost every finding comes from search-result summaries of news reports, not full articles, and several positions were last confirmed months before November 2026.

## In the US, only Google has a documented block on both tools

**Amazon went from restriction to permission.** Its caution is older than its own tool: internal documents from February 2024 said third-party generative AI tools were "strictly prohibited" for confidential work [B] ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/technology/story/amazon-stops-employees-from-using-generative-ai-tools-like-chatgpt-418845-2024-02-25)). In October 2025 Amazon gave Codex the internal label "Do Not Use" [B]. On 24 November 2025 Reuters published an internal memo. It called Amazon's own Kiro "our recommended AI-native development tool for Amazon" and said the company did "not plan to support additional third party, AI development tools" [A, memo text seen through reprints] ([Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/11/25/amazon-pushes-in-house-ai-coding-tool-kiro-over-competitors039-memo-shows)). The memo did not say "ban", and it kept support for tools already in use. Claude Code briefly got the same "Do Not Use" label, and Amazon withdrew it after a reporter asked [B]. In February 2026 Business Insider reported that Claude Code needed formal approval for production code, and that about 1,500 employees had backed an internal thread asking Amazon to adopt it. Amazon answered that there was no explicit ban [B] ([Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/02/12/1530202)). On 4 May 2026 a note from vice president Jim Haughwout made **Claude Code available company-wide at once, with Codex following on 12 May** [B] ([Business Insider repost](https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/04/amazon-employees-pushed-for-claude-code-now-theyre-getting-it-and-codex-too/)). Both run on Amazon's own cloud ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/amazon-coding-agents-developers/)). Kiro remains the default. Kiro itself runs Anthropic's Claude models, and now OpenAI's GPT models too ([Kiro documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/models/); [Kiro model list](https://kiro.dev/docs/models/available-models/)). So Amazon's line was at the outside tool, not at the model. The tools were approved after employees pushed for them and once they ran on Amazon's infrastructure. No change after May 2026 was found.

**Microsoft moved the other way.** In January 2026 The Verge reported that Microsoft told engineers in its CoreAI and Experiences + Devices groups to install Claude Code and compare it with GitHub Copilot [B] ([LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/ai/why-microsoft-engineers-are-using-claude-code)). On 14 May 2026 The Verge reported that Experiences + Devices (Windows, Microsoft 365, Teams, Surface) would **cancel most Claude Code licences by 30 June 2026** and move to GitHub Copilot CLI [B] ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/microsoft-pulls-claude-code-licenses-and-pushes-developers-back-toward-its-own-ai-tool/)). A memo from executive Rajesh Jha said Copilot CLI is a product Microsoft can shape directly with GitHub ([Developer Tech](https://www.developer-tech.com/news/microsoft-claude-code-github-copilot-cli/)). The Verge's sources added that the decision was "also a financial one" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/microsoft-cancels-claude-code-licenses-shifting-developers-to-github-copilot-cli-a-move-likely-driven-by-financial-motives)). No outlet called this a ban. It is a withdrawal of paid licences, and Claude models stay available inside Copilot CLI. Two small sites disagree with the main reports: one says the group is "fully off" Claude Code ([EPC Group](https://www.epcgroup.net/blog/github-copilot-cli-replacing-claude-code-microsoft-engineers-2026)), and another names the replacement as "Copilot Desktop" ([Awesome Agents](https://awesomeagents.ai/news/microsoft-drops-claude-code-copilot-desktop/)). Neither is confirmed elsewhere. No source after 1 July 2026 was found, so it is not confirmed that the cut happened as planned. Nothing was found on Codex or Cursor at Microsoft.

**Google is the one US company with a documented rule against both tools on its own code.** A June 2025 email from engineering vice president Megan Kacholia told engineers to use Google's internal models for coding and pointed them to Cider, an internal tool [B] ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/google-tells-employees-to-use-ai-more-for-coding/496195)). Secondary reports disagree on one detail. Most say the email's approval route was for outside AI in non-coding work. Some headlines say it was for outside coding tools ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/google-engineers-can-no-longer-use-outside-ai-coding-assistants/tldr)). Bloomberg reported on 21 April 2026 that Google **does not let employees use rival tools such as Claude Code and Codex, for security reasons**. Employees can apply for an exception if they show a work need [B] ([Slashdot](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/26/04/21/1655253/googles-internal-politics-leave-it-playing-catch-up-on-ai-coding)). A Google principal engineer, Jaana Dogan, wrote in January 2026 that Claude Code is allowed only for open-source projects [C] ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-engineer-says-claude-code-built-in-one-hour-what-her-team-spent-a-year-on/)). Access was unequal. A former Google engineer claimed in April 2026 that Google's AI adoption was lagging, and later that DeepMind engineers used Claude daily while most of Google did not [C]. Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind called the first post "completely false" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/google-leaders-including-demis-hassabis-push-back-on-claim-of-uneven-ai-adoption-internally)). The narrower point, that Claude was limited to some DeepMind teams and priority projects, is supported by Bloomberg and Business Insider [B]. The change came in September 2026. Business Insider reported on 14 September that Google had made **Claude Opus 5 available to all engineers inside its internal Antigravity tool, on a per-user quota**, after engineers had grown frustrated with Gemini's limits [B] ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-claude-internal-coding-gemini/)). A spokesperson said: "Gemini remains our primary and foundational model for internal development, with third-party models available on a quota to support specialized use cases" [A, quoted in secondary coverage]. The same reports say Google is not opening the Claude Code tool itself. One search for later changes found none.

**Meta started open and is narrowing.** Its internal coding assistant Devmate ran partly on Claude from March 2025 [B] ([Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2506-meta-anthropic-ai-llama-coding-efficiency/)). The Information reported that Meta had roughly **60,000 Claude Code licences** earlier in 2026 and about 30,000 later. This was seen only through small secondary sites, and the date of the cut is unclear [B] ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/shots-fired-meta-is-developers-on-outside-ai-tools-in-half-thats-a-big-problem-for-anthropic)). In late June 2026 The Information reported new limits. Engineers, mainly in the Applied AI division that builds Meta's own tool MetaCode, **may not use Claude Code or Codex output to create training or evaluation data for Meta's models** [B] ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/meta-shuts-engineers-out-of-claude-code-and-codex-over-distillation-fears)). An internal memo warned of "serious escalations with partner companies". Several headlines say Meta "banned" the tools. The rule as described is narrower, and routine use stays allowed. Meta has not commented. Meta has also launched its own coding agent, Muse Code ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4206084/meta-launchs-muse-code-for-complex-software-work-with-persistent-ai-agents.html)). The sources conflict on the model behind MetaCode: Devmate used Claude in 2025, while one 2026 summary says MetaCode runs on Meta's own Muse Spark models [C].

**Apple went from a 2023 restriction to budgeted access.** The Wall Street Journal reported in May 2023 that Apple restricted ChatGPT and told employees not to use GitHub Copilot [B] ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2023/05/19/apple-bans-employees-from-using-chatgpt/)). By May 2025 Apple was rolling out an internal version of Xcode built with Anthropic's Claude [B] ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/05/02/apple-anthropic-ai-coding-platform/)). On 4 November 2026 Bloomberg reported that employees can use Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini under **daily spending caps**, and that Apple cut the cap for some engineers from $300 to $200 in October [B] ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/04/apple-reducing-employee-ai-spending/)). Apple declined to comment. No source says whether the Claude Code or Codex tools are formally approved for Apple's own code. The signs that they are used are indirect and grade C. Claude Code project files were found inside an Apple app in April 2026 ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/apple-claude-code-ai-internal-leak/)). A November 2026 job listing, seen through one blog, asks for "daily use of agentic coding tools such as Claude Code, Codex" ([Mad Robot](https://madrobot.blog/2026/11/20/apple-ai-agent-jobs-claude-code-codex/)).

Three things are common to the five. First, none of them limits engineers to in-house models only. Google came closest, until September 2026. Nor does any of them restrict AI itself: Amazon still wants more than 80% of its developers to use AI every week [B] ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/28/amazon-token-maxxing-ai-work/)), and Google says 75% of its new code is AI-generated and approved by engineers [A] ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/04/24/2026/google-ceo-says-75-of-companys-new-code-is-ai-generated)). Second, the word "ban" in headlines usually means "not approved for production code" or "no company licence". Third, the outside model becomes acceptable once it runs in a place the company controls: on Amazon's cloud, inside Google's tool, or, by one reporter's account, on Apple's own servers ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/01/30/apple-runs-on-anthropic-says-mark-gurman/)).

## In China, subsidies turned into bans within months

**ByteDance acted twice, and neither step is a total ban.** In May 2025 its security department emailed staff that third-party AI coding software such as Cursor and Windsurf would be disabled in batches from 30 June 2025, with its own Trae as the replacement [B] ([Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/102643443.html)). A follow-up notice gave two reasons. Employees were buying tools with personal accounts, so company data built up there. And some tools were not formally offered in China, which is a compliance risk. The notice added that compliant tools could still be applied for ([ifeng](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8jkONUSCAFN)). In April 2026 an internal letter even said the company would repay 50% of what employees spent trying AI products in their own time [B] ([Economic Observer](http://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/0613/913745.shtml)). Then, on 3 and 4 November 2026, Chinese media reported a new rule. **All "L4" code repositories and Feishu (飞书) documents are closed to outside AI, company-wide, including Claude and Codex accounts that employees pay for themselves** [B, no official announcement] ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/11/04/bytedance-reportedly-tightens-access-to-claude-and-codex-even-for-employees-paying-out-of-pocket/)). Some work in the Seed model team is exempt. Public work information and personal use are reportedly not restricted ([ifeng](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8xnW4kmNyTg)). The sources do not agree on the level. The rule is reported as "L4", while the quoted reply from technology vice president Hong Dingkun speaks of "L3 or above" ([TMTPost](https://www.tmtpost.com/8191863.html)).

**Alibaba went from the most open position to the strictest within weeks.** Through June 2026 it repaid staff for Claude, GPT and Gemini use, and Claude Code, Codex and its own Qoder were all everyday tools [B] ([EET-China](https://www.eet-china.com/news/202607033036.html)). Two things then happened. In June, Anthropic accused operators tied to Alibaba's Qwen lab of copying Claude's abilities ("distillation") through nearly 25,000 fake accounts [B] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/anthropic-alibaba-distillation-campaign.html)). Alibaba denies it by most accounts, and one report says it had not responded. On 30 June a developer found that Claude Code checked the user's time zone and proxy settings and put markers into what it sent. An Anthropic employee confirmed this as "an experiment we launched in March" against resellers and distillation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/04/alibaba-reportedly-bans-employees-from-using-claude-code/)). No independent technical check of that code was found. On 3 July, Yicai reported that Alibaba had put **Claude Code on its high-risk software list and would ban it in the office environment (办公环境) from 10 July 2026**, recommending Qoder [B, no on-record statement seen] ([Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2026-07-03/doc-inifpqky0373510.shtml)). Reports disagree on the scope. Yicai's wording covers Claude Code only. Other reports say all Anthropic products and models had to be uninstalled ([EET-China](https://www.eet-china.com/news/202607033036.html)). Reports also disagree on the reason. The stated reason is a security "backdoor" risk. Insiders also pointed to the distillation dispute ([Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260703A0ADSW00?adChannelId=tech)). No Alibaba rule on Codex, Cursor or GitHub Copilot is documented, and nothing dated August to November 2026 confirms that the ban is still in force. Ant Group reportedly disabled "native" Claude Code from 20 July 2026, but this rests on one workplace column [C] ([Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-07-15/doc-inihwrra4767554.shtml)).

**The rest either require approval or have no documented rule.** JD.com has intercepted visits to outside AI model sites since late March 2026. Staff see a page with JD's own model and an application form. Meituan stopped recommending Alibaba's Qwen models by default in April 2026 and asks for a manager's approval [B-] ([ITHome](https://www.ithome.com/0/936/896.htm)). In July 2026 Beijing Youth Daily passed on reports that both had told programmers to "rein in calls to Claude and other external AI models" [B-] ([NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L1B066N20514R9KQ.html)). Both companies apply these controls to Chinese rivals' models too. Tencent has no documented ban. It says over 90% of its engineers use its own CodeBuddy [A, self-reported] ([ifeng](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8niMJjREUb3)). The only evidence on outside tools is one employee's account that Claude Code was taken out of the internal model pool in November 2025 and later put back [B-/C]. Tencent, JD.com and Meituan all declined to comment. Kuaishou reportedly tightened the use of outside coding software in December 2025, based on an anonymous online tip [C] ([Huxiu](https://m.huxiu.com/article/4821062.html)). For Baidu, Huawei and Xiaomi the research found no rule on outside tools. That is an empty result, not evidence that the tools are allowed. Baidu has its own tool, Comate ([Huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4874047.html)), and Xiaomi open-sourced its own MiMo Code in June 2026 ([Eastmoney](https://caifuhao.eastmoney.com/news/20260611205316030926710)). NetEase, PDD, Didi, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu were not searched.

**In-house tool does not mean in-house model in China either.** ByteDance's Trae still listed GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro for international users after it dropped Claude on 4 November 2025 ([AIBase](https://news.aibase.com/news/22507)). Meituan's CatPaw supports a mix of models, including competitors' ([Huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4846107.html)). The reverse also exists. Developer tutorials show the Claude Code program running on Chinese models such as DeepSeek, Qwen and Kimi [C as evidence of practice] ([CSDN](https://blog.csdn.net/apo0625/article/details/150975524)). No big company has said whether it allows this, and the July 2026 actions target the Claude Code program itself.

## Vendors and governments also restrict access from outside

**For Chinese companies the first "ban" comes from the vendor.** On 4 September 2025 Anthropic stopped serving any entity "more than 50% owned, directly or indirectly" by companies headquartered in unsupported regions such as China, "regardless of where they operate" [A] ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions)). A rewritten policy took effect on 12 November 2026. It excludes anyone physically in an unsupported region, any entity incorporated or headquartered there, and any entity majority-owned or controlled from there. It covers Claude.ai, Claude Code, the API and Claude sold through cloud platforms and resellers [A] ([Anthropic Supported Regions Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/supported-countries)). Mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Russia are not on the supported list ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/supported-regions)). The first clause also covers employees of foreign companies while they are physically in those places. After the 2025 rule, Chinese-owned tools dropped Claude: Tencent's CodeBuddy international edition, then ByteDance's Trae. Media tied this to Anthropic's rule, and Trae itself gave no such reason [B] ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3331638/tech-war-bytedance-cuts-claude-model-after-anthropic-restricts-china-access)). Anthropic also says it has disrupted distillation campaigns from seven China-based labs, including Alibaba and Xiaomi. The figures are Anthropic's own. For Alibaba they differ between its June and September 2026 accounts, and nobody has verified them independently [A for the statement only] ([Anthropic threat report](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)). OpenAI took a narrower step earlier. It told API users in unsupported regions, including mainland China and Hong Kong, that their traffic would be blocked from 9 July 2024 [B] ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/06/25/openai_unsupported_countries/)). Where Codex is officially available was not verified.

**Vendors also cut off rivals.** Anthropic's commercial terms forbid customers to "access the Services to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models" [A] ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). Anthropic revoked OpenAI's API access in August 2025 and blocked xAI staff from Claude inside Cursor in January 2026 [B] ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/08/02/anthropic-cuts-off-openais-access-to-its-claude-models); [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/report-anthropic-cuts-off-xais-access-to-its-models-for-coding/)). OpenAI did the same in reverse. In August 2026 it gave notice that it would stop supplying models to Cursor after SpaceX bought it, with a proposed shutoff on 12 November 2026 [B] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/29/openai-cursor-spacex-model-access.html)). What happened on that date is unverified. No report was found of Anthropic cutting off Google, Meta, Microsoft or Amazon. Read literally, though, the clause means that an engineer who uses Claude Code to help train a rival model breaks the contract. That fits Meta's June 2026 rule and its warning about "partner companies". A third vendor rule matters less to large companies. Since early 2026 Anthropic and Google stop people from using a flat-rate subscription login inside third-party tools ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/23/google_antigravity_compute_burden/)). Companies that pay through the API or a cloud account are not affected.

**Governments acted against a tool or a vendor three times in 2026.** In China, the industry ministry's vulnerability platform warned on 8 July 2026 that Claude Code versions 2.1.91 to 2.1.196 contain a "security backdoor" that sends region and identity data without consent. It told users to uninstall or upgrade [A, a government notice read through media reprints] ([Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-07/08/content_544161.html)). Sources differ on the exact version numbers. This is a risk warning, not a law, and Alibaba's ban was reported five days before it. Chinese authorities, including the same platform, had warned in March 2026 about another agent tool, OpenClaw ([Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260310/959f13d18edb4759ae031a5e30523d23/c.html)). In the US, President Trump directed federal agencies on 27 February 2026 to stop using Anthropic's technology [B] ([Reuters via Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/world/2026/02/28/trump-directs-us-agencies-to-toss-anthropics-ai-as-pentagon-calls-startup-a-supply-chain-risk/210689)). The Defense Department named Anthropic a supply chain risk after the company refused to drop two limits on how the military may use Claude [A for Anthropic's own account] ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/where-stand-department-war)). The courts split. A California district court ruled for Anthropic, and sources disagree on whether its first win came in March or in August. On 25 September 2026 a Washington appeals court upheld a separate Pentagon designation by 2 votes to 1 [B] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/pentagon-anthropic-ai-risk-appeals-court.html)). The effect on big technology companies is narrow. Claude is kept out of work done for the Defense Department, and Microsoft, Google and Amazon said in March 2026 that it stays available to their other customers ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/despite-pentagon-ban-google-aws-and-microsoft-stick-with-anthropics-ai-models/)). Civilian agencies buy Claude under an agreement announced on 27 October 2026 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa)). The third case hit every customer. A US export-control order of 12 June 2026 closed Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models to foreign nationals. Anthropic had "no reliable way to verify nationality in real-time", so it **switched both models off for all customers until the order was lifted on 30 June** [A] ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)). Reuters reported that this pushed large European companies to spread their AI use across several providers ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/analysisus-curbs-on-ai-spur-european-firms-to-spread-the-risk-4700367)). No European company was found that bans US coding tools on sovereignty grounds, but rules in the EU, India and Russia were not searched properly.

## Security is the stated reason; rivalry and cost explain the timing

**Each company gives a security or data reason.** Google cites security. ByteDance cites data leaks through personal accounts, and compliance. Alibaba cites a backdoor risk. JD.com cites leaks of business data. Apple's 2023 rule was about confidential data. Meta's concern is different: output from a rival's tool ends up in the training data of Meta's own models. The best-known case outside this group followed a real leak. Samsung banned generative AI in May 2023 after engineers put internal source code into ChatGPT [B] ([Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-02/samsung-bans-chatgpt-and-other-generative-ai-use-by-staff-after-leak)).

**The second reason is rarely stated but fits the pattern: the company has its own tool to promote.** Almost every restriction comes with an in-house replacement: Kiro, Copilot CLI, Antigravity, MetaCode, Trae, Qoder. The Verge wrote that Claude Code had "undermined" Microsoft's Copilot CLI ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/microsoft-cancels-claude-code-licenses-shifting-developers-to-github-copilot-cli-a-move-likely-driven-by-financial-motives)). ByteDance's technology vice president denied this motive on the intranet: "promoting a product does not need this method of banning other choices and forcing use, because it works badly" ([TMTPost](https://www.tmtpost.com/8191863.html)). JD.com and Meituan show that security is not the whole story, because they also restrict the models of Chinese competitors. In-house tools are not automatically the safer choice either. Press reports linked two AWS outages, one of them in December 2025, to Amazon's own Kiro and Amazon Q, not to outside tools. Amazon called it "user error, not AI error" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/amazon_denies_kiro_agentic_ai_behind_outage/)).

**Cost became the third reason in 2026.** Microsoft's decision was partly financial. Apple is cutting daily caps. Tencent cut employee token quotas from June 2026 [B/C] ([Economic Observer](http://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/0613/913745.shtml)). Uber spent its 2026 AI budget in about four months [B], and a spokesperson then confirmed a cap of $1,500 per employee per month for each coding tool [A] ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-02/uber-caps-usage-of-ai-tools-like-claude-code-to-cut-costs)). A cap is not a ban. It assumes the tool is approved.

**Almost every rule has exceptions.** At Google these were open-source work [C], a shown work need, and, before September 2026, some DeepMind teams and priority projects [B]. At ByteDance they are some Seed team work, public work information, personal use, and an application route for compliant tools. Amazon's 2025 memo kept tools already in use, and teams could apply for approval. At Microsoft "most" licences end, which implies that some stay, and no source says whose. At Meta routine tasks stay allowed. At Apple senior managers can grant higher spending limits. JD.com and Meituan have an application channel. Alibaba's rule is worded for the "office environment", and Ant's reported rule names "native" Claude Code. What those words leave open is not documented.

**Enforcement differs by country.** In the US cases it works through approval lists, licences and budgets. No report of network blocking or punishment over these tools was found, though the targeted searches for this could not be run. In China it is more technical: a software blacklist (Alibaba), network interception (JD.com) and access control by data class (ByteDance). No publicised case was found of a big-tech engineer dismissed only for using Claude Code, Cursor or Codex. The one on-record case is in ByteDance's September 2026 discipline bulletin. An employee used AI tools to compile confidential documents and backed them up, with code, to a personal account [A] ([NBD](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-09-22/4588970.html)).

## Companies without a leading coding product buy the tools, then limit the spending

Among Western internet and software companies that do not sell a leading coding assistant, the research found adoption wherever it found anything, and no ban. Shopify's chief executive called AI use "a baseline expectation" in 2025, and its engineers can use Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and GitHub Copilot [A] ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/04/08/internal-memo-shopify-ceo-declares-ai-non-optional/); [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering)). Coinbase's chief executive said he fired engineers who would not start using Cursor and GitHub Copilot [A] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/22/coinbase-ceo-explains-why-he-fired-engineers-who-didnt-try-ai-immediately)). Nvidia's chief executive said in October 2025 that all its software engineers use Cursor [A, may be outdated] ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/10/15/jensen-huang-says-every-nvidia-engineer-now-codes-with-cursor)). Stripe deployed Claude Code to 1,370 engineers, according to a vendor case study ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/customers/stripe)). Spotify's internal system is built on Claude Code [A] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/12/spotify-says-its-best-developers-havent-written-a-line-of-code-since-december-thanks-to-ai/)). A Netflix engineer described Claude Code, Cursor and Codex in use side by side in November 2026 [A] ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/netflix-sdlc-ai-coding-agents/)). Accenture agreed in December 2025 to give Claude Code to tens of thousands of its developers [A] ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-accenture-partnership)).

**Owning an AI product does not predict a ban in this group.** Salesforce sells its own coding product and still made Cursor a default tool for its engineers [A] ([Salesforce Engineering](https://engineering.salesforce.com/building-sustainable-enterprise-ai-adoption-cultural-strategies-that-achieved-95-developer-engagement/)). ServiceNow rolled out Claude Code company-wide, according to a vendor announcement ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/servicenow-anthropic-claude)). IBM is the closest to "in-house first". It reports more than 80,000 employees on its own tool, Bob, but Bob runs partly on Claude, and IBM's rule on outside tools is unknown [A] ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/10/02/ibm-brings-its-bob-ai-coding-agent-inside-firewalls-for-regulated-companies/)). Tesla is the one case that looks like favouring a related company. It capped third-party AI tools at $200 a week from July 2026 and exempted products of its affiliate xAI [B] ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spending-200-week/)).

**The clearest reversals are in South Korea.** Samsung lifted its 2023 ban in mid-2026. On 21 June 2026 OpenAI announced ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex for all Samsung Electronics employees in Korea and all staff of its device division worldwide [A, vendor announcement] ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/samsung-electronics-chatgpt-codex-deployment/)). Sources disagree on whether the chip division is included. LG signed a group-wide Claude Enterprise contract that is reported to include Claude Code [A] ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10767160)). SK hynix still restricts outside AI agents and is reviewing ChatGPT Enterprise [A] ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10770334)). All three have their own AI models or platforms. Their restrictions came from fear of leaks, not from a rival product. In Japan, Mercari and Rakuten both use Claude Code in development [A, company and vendor material] ([Mercari Engineering](https://engineering.mercari.com/en/blog/entry/20251030-taming-agents-in-the-mercari-web-monorepo/); [Anthropic](https://claude.com/customers/rakuten)).

**Where limits exist in this group, they are about money and role.** Uber and Tesla capped spending. Accenture has centrally funded only about $29 of Claude use per employee per month since October 2026 and charges the rest to projects [B] ([404 Media](https://404media.co/accenture-tells-employees-they-can-now-spend-only-29-on-claude-per-month/)). Accenture says the $29 is not a hard limit ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/10/16/accenture-bets-big-on-claude-then-limits-ai-spending/)). Goldman Sachs gave Claude Code to about 12,000 engineers and in October 2026 removed coding tools from traders and bankers [B] ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/goldman-sachs-pulls-ai-coding-tools-from-traders-and-bankers/)). Surveys point the same way, though most come from security vendors with products to sell. In 2023, 75% of organisations were "considering or implementing" bans on generative AI ([BlackBerry](https://blogs.blackberry.com/en/2023/08/why-companies-ban-chatgpt-ai)). In 2024, 15% prohibited AI code generation, and 99% saw it used anyway ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/15-of-companies-ban-code-ai-but-99-of-developers-use-it-anyway/)). In October 2026 only 6.1% of developers in the Stack Overflow survey said their organisation does not use AI tools ([Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-annual-developer-survey-ai-trust-conditional)). These surveys asked different questions, so they show a direction, not a rate.

## What this research could not check

Three limits apply to the whole report. First, the researchers shared a budget of 200 web searches, and it ran out before every planned search was run. Second, the network settings of the research environment blocked opening news articles in full. Only Anthropic's own pages (policies, announcements, documentation) were opened and read. Almost every other finding rests on search-result summaries and headlines, cross-checked across several outlets where possible. Quotes are as given in those summaries and were not checked against the original articles. Many links point to secondary coverage of paywalled originals (Business Insider, Bloomberg, The Information, The Wall Street Journal). Third, the grades A, B and C describe the type of source, not a verified reading of it.

Dates matter. Only three positions were confirmed in November 2026 itself: Apple (4 November), ByteDance (3 to 4 November) and Anthropic's regional policy (12 November). Google was last confirmed in September, Alibaba in July, Meta between June and September, Amazon and Microsoft in May. Searches for later changes at Amazon, Microsoft and Google found none. That is weak evidence that nothing changed.

Several questions were not researched or returned nothing. At Amazon: GitHub Copilot, and Cursor in 2026. At Microsoft: Codex, Cursor, separate rules at GitHub and LinkedIn, and the history before 2026. At Google: other Alphabet units, and whether DeepMind teams keep direct Claude Code access. At Meta: Cursor, Copilot and Gemini, and when Claude Code and Codex were first approved. At Apple: whether Claude Code and Codex are formally approved, and any enforcement. In China: Huawei (two searches found nothing), Baidu and Xiaomi (no rule found), NetEase, PDD, Didi, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu (not searched), all positions in 2023 and 2024, and any confirmation after July 2026 for Alibaba, Ant, JD.com, Meituan and Tencent. On the vendor side: OpenAI's terms on competitors, where Codex and Google's tools are officially available, and whether OpenAI's Cursor shutoff happened. On governments: the EU, India and Russia. In the comparison group, about 30 named companies returned nothing, among them DoorDash, Lyft, eBay, PayPal, Adobe, Intel, Sony, Toyota, NTT, Coupang, Flipkart, Grab, Bosch, Booking.com and Zalando. Two possible reasons for restriction were not searched: copyright and open-source licence risk, and client contracts at outsourcing firms.

Where the note files or their sources disagree, the report says so in place. One apparent gap was settled by a primary source: one note file could not confirm the scope of the June 2026 US export order, and Anthropic's own posts, which were opened, describe it. One bias runs through everything. Companies announce adoption and stay quiet about bans, so the public record understates restriction.

## Conclusion

The question assumes two camps: companies that ban outside tools and companies that allow them. The evidence shows a different dividing line. What large companies defend is the tool around the model and the place where their code and prompts go, not the model itself. Amazon accepted Claude Code and Codex once both ran on its own cloud. Google accepted Claude once it ran inside Google's own tool. ByteDance now sorts by how secret the data is, not by which tool is used. In the US this has produced a shared middle position from two directions: outside models are welcome, outside tools are limited, and the company's own tool is the default. China spent 2026 moving the other way, pushed by a vendor that refuses to serve Chinese companies and a regulator that distrusts that vendor's tool.

Two things follow. The choice is less and less the employer's alone. A vendor's contract clause, an export order or a court ruling can remove a tool or a model within days, which is why large companies keep an in-house fallback and more than one supplier. And the next limit is the budget, not the ban: Apple, Uber, Tesla, Accenture and Tencent all cut or capped AI spending in 2026. For an engineer, the practical test before using Claude Code or Codex at work has three parts: does the company provide it, which code may it touch, and who pays.
