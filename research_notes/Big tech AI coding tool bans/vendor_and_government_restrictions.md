## 1. Anthropic and China: the ownership rule, its enforcement through 2026, and where Claude and Claude Code are available

### Takeaway
The rule dates from 4 September 2025 (entities more than 50% owned, directly or indirectly, by companies headquartered in unsupported regions are barred wherever they operate) and has since been tightened, not relaxed. The Supported Regions Policy in force since 12 November 2026 excludes anyone physically in an unsupported region, any entity incorporated or headquartered there, and any entity majority-owned or controlled from there, across Claude.ai, Claude Code, Cowork, the API and Claude on cloud platforms and resellers; mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Russia are not on the supported list.

### Cited Findings

**The September 2025 rule {model, API, subscription, cloud}**
- 4 Sep 2025 [A]: Anthropic's post says the update "prohibits companies or organizations whose ownership structures" subject them to control from jurisdictions where its products are not permitted, and that this includes "entities that are more than 50% owned, directly or indirectly" by companies headquartered in unsupported regions, "regardless of where they operate". — [Anthropic, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions)
- Stated reasons [A]: companies from restricted regions kept access "through subsidiaries incorporated in other countries"; such companies can be compelled "to share data, cooperate with intelligence services"; their use could "ultimately serve adversarial military and intelligence services and broader authoritarian objectives"; and they could "advance their own AI development through techniques like distillation". — [Anthropic, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions)
- The post names no company and names only China as an example; it does not name Russia, Iran or North Korea, and it gives no effective date [A]. — [Anthropic, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions). A parallel note in this project records the rule as effective 5 Sep 2025, citing SCMP (x-ref). — [SCMP, Sep 2025](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3324504/tech-war-us-start-anthropic-blocks-chinese-firms-subsidiaries-worldwide-ai-access)
- Discrepancy: The Decoder's headline describes the rule as banning companies "majority controlled by China, Russia, Iran and North Korea"; Anthropic's text names only China [B†]. — [The Decoder, Sep 2025](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-bans-companies-majority-controlled-by-china-russia-iran-and-north-korea-from-claude/)
- Scope as reported in September 2025 [B†]: the rule applies wherever the entity operates and covers direct customers and organisations reaching Claude through cloud services; SCMP called it an escalation from earlier measures that only targeted companies located in China and said it was unclear how many entities were affected. — [SCMP, Sep 2025](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3324504/tech-war-us-start-anthropic-blocks-chinese-firms-subsidiaries-worldwide-ai-access)

**The 2026 rewrite: Supported Regions Policy effective 12 November 2026 {model, tool, API, subscription, cloud}**
- 8 Oct 2026 [A]: Anthropic announced a "2026 Usage Policy update" taking effect 12 November and said the Supported Regions page was updated "to clarify how we enforce this policy"; the post restates that the policy prohibits use by people physically located in an unsupported region, by entities incorporated or headquartered in one, and by entities majority-owned or controlled by persons or entities there. — [Anthropic, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)
- Policy text, effective 12 Nov 2026 [A]: products are "available only in the countries and regions listed below"; scope is Anthropic's apps (Claude.ai, Claude Code and Cowork), its API and developer platform, and "Claude models offered through cloud platforms and authorized resellers"; "Any country or region not listed is unsupported." — [Anthropic, Supported Regions Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/supported-countries); [same text at /legal/supported-regions](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions)
- Three exclusions [A]: (1) use "while physically located in an unsupported region", including by users and personnel of otherwise supported entities; (2) entities "incorporated or headquartered in an unsupported region", with their users and personnel "regardless of whether such individuals are physically located in a supported region"; (3) entities "majority-owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by persons or entities in unsupported regions". The page does not define "control", states no exceptions and describes no enforcement mechanics. — [Anthropic, Supported Regions Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/supported-countries)
- Usage Policy, effective 12 Nov 2026 [A]: prohibits providing access in unsupported regions in violation of the Supported Regions Policy (for the customer and the end users it serves), creating multiple accounts "to avoid detection", and circumventing "a ban through the use of a different account"; enforcement may include warnings, throttling, suspension or termination. — [Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- Commercial Terms (effective 17 Jun 2025, live on 26 Nov 2026) [A]: section D.2 requires compliance with "our policy on the countries and regions Anthropic currently supports" and says the customer "must cooperate with reasonable requests for information from Anthropic to verify Customer's identity and use of the Services"; section I.3.a lets Anthropic suspend access for violations of D.1, D.2 or D.4. — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Secondary coverage of 12–13 Nov 2026 describes the rewrite as closing three loopholes and as applying to people using the service while physically in mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau even if their accounts are based in supported regions [C†, consistent with the primary text]. — [Technology.org, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/13/anthropic-claude-supported-regions-policy-china-controlled-entities/); [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/55286); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/b7798d52-d5c2-499b-9c98-59d53c2b7688)
- Meduza reportedly noted that the ownership limits were not new in November 2026 and that a version of the supported-regions documentation dated 12 May 2026 carried the same country list [C†, relayed by an aggregator]. — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2303466)

### Inferences
- The 2026 text is broader than the 2025 text in three ways: owners can be "persons or entities" (2025: "companies headquartered"), "controlled" is added alongside majority ownership, and physical presence in an unsupported region is itself disqualifying even for staff of supported companies. The 2025 post already covered cloud access per reporting; the 2026 policy says so in its own words.
- For a Chinese internet group this closes every first-party and cloud route to Claude models for all subsidiaries worldwide. What remains lawful is the Claude Code harness pointed at a non-Claude model (see section 10), which the July 2026 Chinese warning and Alibaba's ban then targeted from the other side.
- The physical-location clause also bites on non-Chinese multinationals: employees working from mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau offices fall outside the policy even on a compliant corporate account. Goldman's Hong Kong-only restriction is consistent with this, but its reason is not documented.

### Gaps
- The policy's version history and PDF could not be opened (assets.claude.com unreachable), so I cannot date each wording change or say exactly what "clarify how we enforce" changed.
- No published count of accounts or organisations closed on regional or ownership grounds; no named enterprise confirmed on record as cut off under the ownership test.
- Whether Trae's loss of Claude was Anthropic's act or ByteDance's choice is unresolved.
- How AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft enforce the ownership test on Bedrock, Vertex AI and Foundry was not found.
- Which "overseas tools backed by China" SCMP listed in September 2025 was not read. Unverified recollection only: Chinese-founded, Singapore-based agent startups were discussed in this context.

## 2. Anthropic and distillation: what was claimed and what restrictions followed

### Takeaway
The lead is confirmed and has grown: Anthropic named DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax on 23 February 2026 (about 24,000 fraudulent accounts, over 16 million exchanges), accused Alibaba in June 2026, and by September 2026 said it had disrupted campaigns from seven China-based labs. What followed were account-level and product-level restrictions (bans, identity checks, classifiers, summarised reasoning, limits on new API accounts, charging for blocked requests) plus a US policy track, not lawsuits.

### Cited Findings
- 23 Feb 2026 [A]: "over 16 million exchanges with Claude through approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts", in violation of the terms of service and regional access restrictions. DeepSeek: over 150,000 exchanges (reasoning, rubric-based grading, censorship-safe rewrites). Moonshot AI: over 3.4 million (agentic reasoning, tool use, coding, computer use; later attempts to reconstruct reasoning traces). MiniMax: over 13 million (agentic coding, tool use), redirecting nearly half its traffic within 24 hours of a new Claude release. — [Anthropic, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- Access route [A]: "commercial proxy services which resell access to Claude and other frontier AI models at scale", running "hydra cluster" networks of fraudulent accounts across the API and third-party cloud platforms; one proxy network "managed more than 20,000 fraudulent accounts simultaneously" and mixed distillation traffic with unrelated customer requests. — [Anthropic, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- Response announced in February [A]: classifiers and behavioural fingerprinting; "We are sharing technical indicators with other AI labs, cloud providers, and relevant authorities"; "strengthened verification for educational accounts, security research programs, and startup organizations"; product, API and model-level countermeasures. The post mentions no legal action and argues distillation undermines export controls. — [Anthropic, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- Coverage reported the figures as Anthropic's allegations; I found no independent verification and no response from the three labs in the results [B†]. — [Tom's Hardware, Feb 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-accuses-deepseek-other-chinese-ai-developers-of-industrial-scale-copying-claims-distillation-included-24-000-fraudulent-accounts-and-16-million-exchanges-to-train-smaller-models)
- 9 Jun 2026 {model} [A]: on Claude Fable 5, requests flagged by classifiers as distillation (or cyber, biology and chemistry) are automatically answered by Claude Opus 4.8 instead. — [Anthropic, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5)
- June 2026, Alibaba [B†]: in a 10 Jun 2026 letter to the Senate Banking Committee, Anthropic said operators tied to Alibaba's Qwen lab generated more than 28.8 million exchanges through nearly 25,000 fraudulent accounts between 22 April and 5 June 2026, the largest campaign it had disclosed; Alibaba denies the allegations and the figures are not independently verified. — [CNBC, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/anthropic-alibaba-distillation-campaign.html); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/112361/anthropic-says-alibaba-used-25000-fake-accounts-to-query-claude-28-8-million-times-and-train-qwen-off-the-results/index.html)
- 10 Sep 2026 [A]: Anthropic's threat report says that since February it has disrupted campaigns from seven China-based labs, attributed "with high confidence": Alibaba (over 151 million exchanges, May–July 2026, peaking near 3 million a day from more than 3,500 accounts, to train Qwen 3.5–3.7); Moonshot (5,380 accounts, over 23 million exchanges, May–July 2026); DeepSeek (over 12.1 million exchanges over 14 days in July 2026); Zhipu (over 3.4 million over 17 days, June–July 2026); Xiaomi (over 400,000 requests across more than 1,500 accounts, March–April 2026); SenseTime (bought Claude transcripts from data vendors); MiniMax (ran a proxy service through a shell company). It says it has not observed attempts against Mythos 5 or Mythos Preview, although one distillation case involved a Fable or Mythos-class model. — [Anthropic threat intelligence report, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)
- Methods alleged in September [A]: proxy services ("transfer stations") creating thousands of accounts with false identities, fake or stolen cards and stolen API keys; Alibaba used a pool of nearly 5,000 accounts and moved to a second pool when the first was banned; labs bought transcripts from resellers that save conversations without users' knowledge; some labs rerouted their own users' requests to Claude without consent. The report does not say the Chinese government directed the campaigns. — [Anthropic threat intelligence report, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)
- Contradiction: the June letter (28.8 million exchanges, about 25,000 accounts, 22 Apr–5 Jun) and the September report (over 151 million exchanges, May–July, peak from 3,500+ accounts, a 5,000-account pool) give different Alibaba figures for overlapping periods; they may measure different windows and pools. — [CNBC, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/anthropic-alibaba-distillation-campaign.html); [Anthropic, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)
- 12 Oct 2026 [A]: Anthropic says new defences cut data harvested by distillers by roughly 90–99%; "tens of thousands" of distillation-linked accounts were banned, often within two hours; proxy networks now control "hundreds of thousands" of fraudulent accounts; it has turned away "trillions of tokens' worth of paying traffic". It alleges Zhipu distilled cyber capabilities for GLM-5.3 and that requests to Moonshot's Kimi models were secretly forwarded to Claude. — [Anthropic, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses)
- Restrictions that reach ordinary customers {API, model} [A]: "preserved thinking" stops API accounts created on or after 31 Aug 2026 from editing context that precedes Claude's reasoning; reasoning is summarised; with Claude Sonnet 5.5 reasoning transcripts are visible only to the requesting organisation; repeat extractors get stricter limits and bans; since 24 Sep 2026 requests blocked by safeguards are charged in areas with a false-positive rate below 0.1%. Anthropic concedes some legitimate users have been blocked or charged. — [Anthropic, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses)
- Contract basis [A]: the Usage Policy prohibits using outputs to train an AI model ("model scraping", "model distillation") without authorisation; the help centre says "Our Terms do not allow the use of Outputs to train models that are competitive with Anthropic's own" (updated 16 Mar 2026). — [Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup); [Anthropic Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12326764-can-i-use-my-outputs-to-train-an-ai-model)
- Chinese reaction: China's Ministry of Commerce rejected US distillation accusations as lacking factual and legal basis [B†]. — [Just Security, 2026](https://www.justsecurity.org/137498/diagnosis-deterrence-us-response-distillation/). Alibaba banned Claude Code from 10 Jul 2026 and a Chinese regulator issued a warning on the tool (section 9).
- A Hacker News thread titled "Anthropic is accusing my company of distilling. How can I prove I'm not?" exists; I saw only the title [C†]. — [aggregator copy](https://ai.lounge.ai/news/ask-hn-anthropic-is-accusing-my-company-of-distilling-how-can-i-prove-i-m-not)

### Inferences
- The enforcement burden has shifted from named labs to everyone: identity checks, limits on new API accounts and charged blocks apply to all customers, which is how a dispute with seven Chinese labs becomes a constraint on unrelated enterprises.
- Anthropic's own account implies the regional ban is leaky at scale (hundreds of thousands of fraudulent accounts, purchased transcripts), which explains the move from account bans to model-level countermeasures.

### Gaps
- All volume and attribution figures are Anthropic's; none is independently verified. Responses from DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax, Zhipu, Xiaomi and SenseTime were not found.
- Anthropic's Senate Banking letter itself was not located on its site; I rely on CNBC's report, which I could not open.
- No evidence found of Anthropic suing any lab over distillation.

## 3. Anthropic and competing labs or tools: Windsurf, OpenAI, xAI, later cases, and what the terms say

### Takeaway
Three cut-offs are established at grade B: Windsurf's first-party Claude capacity (June 2025), OpenAI's API access (reported 1 August 2025) and xAI staff's Claude access through Cursor (January 2026). I found no later Anthropic cut-off of a Western competitor through November 2026; the contractual basis is section D.4 of the Commercial Terms, which bars using the services to build competing products or train competing AI models.

## 10. Responses: how affected companies adapted

### Takeaway
Affected companies swapped the model, kept the harness, or built their own tool: Chinese tools replaced Claude with other models (Trae with GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro), developers pointed Claude Code at domestic Anthropic-compatible endpoints, a reseller and proxy market grew that Anthropic now treats as a distillation channel, and Alibaba moved staff to its in-house Qoder. Elsewhere the response was diversification across vendors.
