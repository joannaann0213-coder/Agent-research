# Chinese big tech and external AI coding tools: policy fact file (state as of 26 November 2026)

Scope: how ByteDance, Alibaba, Ant Group, Tencent, Baidu, Huawei, Meituan, JD.com, Kuaishou, Xiaomi and others treat their own engineers' use of external AI coding tools (Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, Windsurf, GitHub Copilot) and foreign models, compared with in-house tools and domestic models.

**Research conditions that limit confidence (read first)**

- 31 web searches were completed, in Chinese and English. Three more (the smaller companies; the Claude Code detection controversy; Anthropic's distillation accusation against Alibaba) were refused because the session's shared search budget ran out. Several planned checks were therefore never run; they are listed under Gaps.
- Page fetching failed for every host tried (technode.com, thenextweb.com, finance.sina.com.cn, tech.ifeng.com, juejin.cn are outside the session's allowed network). **No full article text was read.** Every finding rests on search-result titles and summaries, cross-checked across outlets where possible. Where a summary did not tie a fact to one URL, the candidate URLs are given and marked "carrier uncertain".
- Events dated July to November 2026 are known only from these search results.

**Conventions**

- Restriction levels: total ban / banned for proprietary or production code only / officially unsupported or discouraged / approval required / allowed under an enterprise agreement / encouraged or mandated.
- Evidence grades: A = on-record company or government statement, or published memo text; B = reputable outlet citing internal documents or several employees; C = anecdote (forums, blogs, social media, workplace-gossip columns). Grade C items are leads, not facts.
- "May be outdated" = not reconfirmed in any source dated after mid-2026.
- Tool and model are kept separate. Tool (harness): Claude Code, Codex CLI, Cursor, Windsurf, GitHub Copilot, Trae, Qoder, CodeBuddy. Model: Claude, GPT, Gemini, or a domestic model.

## 1. ByteDance (字节跳动): the 2025 Cursor and Windsurf ban, Trae, Claude inside Trae, and the November 2026 rule

### Takeaway
ByteDance has restricted external tools twice, and neither step is a blanket ban on all external AI. In May 2025 an internal security email said third-party AI coding software such as Cursor and Windsurf would be disabled in batches from 30 June 2025, with Trae as the substitute and an application route for compliant tools (grade B). In late October and November 2026 it moved to a rule based on data classification: all L4 code repositories and Feishu (飞书) documents are closed to external AI, including Claude and Codex accounts that employees pay for themselves, while public work information and personal use are reportedly unaffected (grade B; no official announcement found).

### Cited Findings

**The 2025 ban**
- 28 May 2025: Yicai (第一财经) reported that ByteDance's Security and Risk Control department (安全与风控部门) emailed staff that, "to guard against potential data-leak risks" (为防范潜在的数据泄露风险), third-party AI coding software including Cursor and Windsurf would be disabled internally in batches from 30 June 2025, with ByteDance's own Trae named as the alternative. ByteDance had not responded when the story ran. Level: ban of the named third-party tools, phased. Grade B. May be outdated as a description of 2026 practice. — [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/102643443.html); [ITHome (IT之家), 28 May 2025](https://www.ithome.com/0/856/577.htm); [36Kr newsflash](https://www.36kr.com/newsflashes/3311843298008576); [TMTPost English, 28 May 2025](https://en.tmtpost.com/news/7579576)
- Follow-up notice (reported around June 2025): the same department issued a supplementary internal explanation that apologised for the first email's unclear wording and gave two reasons. First, many employees had bought these tools through personal accounts, so company data accumulated in personal accounts in breach of data-security rules. Second, some tools and models were not formally offered in the China region (the search summary renders this as the company's "ZG region" and as 中国区), which is a compliance risk. It added that third-party tools that comply with laws and support tenant management and data control could still be applied for after review, although most staff were steered to Trae. Level: approval required for compliant third-party tools; personal-account use barred. Grade B. May be outdated. — [ifeng Tech](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8jkONUSCAFN); possibly also [Tencent News, 9 June 2025](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250609A08RVC00) (carrier uncertain)
- Reaction (grade C): developers complained that Trae was clearly weaker than Cursor ("if forced to use Trae, I would rather not use AI"), and some argued the data-security reason was weak because Cursor's enterprise edition has a privacy mode. Industry observers speculated that the ban also served to promote Trae. Carrier uncertain between the 2025 items. — [Tencent News, 9 June 2025](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250609A08RVC00); [ITHome](https://www.ithome.com/0/856/577.htm)

**Trae and the models inside it**

- Tool versus model: after dropping Claude, the international Trae still listed two foreign models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) next to two domestic ones. Carrier uncertain: the figure appeared in the summary of the result set containing the two items linked here. — [TechNode, 4 November 2026](https://technode.com/2026/11/04/bytedance-reportedly-tightens-access-to-claude-and-codex-even-for-employees-paying-out-of-pocket/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-ai-coding-tools-claude-code-backdoor)

- Discipline (grade A, the company's own bulletin): see section 5. — [NBD (每日经济新闻), 22 September 2026](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-09-22/4588970.html)

### Inferences
- The November 2026 rule implies that the 2025 ban did not end external-tool use: employees were evidently running Claude and Codex on internal material with self-paid accounts in 2026, and the April 2026 reimbursement policy subsidised external AI products.
- ByteDance has shifted from a tool blacklist (Cursor, Windsurf) to a control by data class (L3 or L4 content cannot reach any external AI). Under this logic the harness matters less than where the data goes.
- The Seed exemption suggests the model-research organisation still needs external models, probably for evaluation; the sources do not say why.
- The 2025 follow-up notice's reference to the China region suggests a China-centred rationale, but this does not establish the scope.

### Gaps
- Scope for TikTok and overseas staff: not established for 2025 or 2026. "Company-wide" in the 2026 reports is not broken down by entity.
- Exact wording of the November 2026 notice. One TMTPost repost is tagged with "keys", "servers" and "explicit marking" (密钥, 服务器, 显式标识), but the details were not retrievable. — [NetEase repost](https://www.163.com/dy/article/LATH36Q805118O92.html)
- Internal use of Doubao Seed Code and successors, and ByteDance's share of AI-generated code: no sourced information found. Unverified recollection only: Doubao-Seed-Code launched in November 2025 with an Anthropic-compatible API and was promoted for use with Claude Code.
- Whether any ByteDance team holds an approved enterprise licence for an external tool in 2026.
- Unverified lead: a Sina-indexed Weibo item says the founder stated he would "rather fall behind than distil". — [Sina Weibo index](https://www.sina.cn/weibo/detail/5361811232655797.html)

## 2. Alibaba (阿里巴巴) and Ant Group (蚂蚁集团)

### Takeaway
Alibaba moved from the most permissive documented position to the most restrictive within about a week, by EET-China's account. Through June 2026 it reimbursed staff for Claude, GPT and Gemini usage, and Claude Code and Codex were everyday tools next to its own Qoder. From 10 July 2026 Claude Code is on Alibaba's high-risk software list and banned in the office environment, with Qoder recommended (grade B: Yicai citing insiders, widely re-reported, no on-record statement found). Ant Group reportedly followed from 20 July 2026 (grade C). No source documents an Alibaba rule on Codex, Cursor or GitHub Copilot.

## 3. Tencent (腾讯)

### Takeaway
No formal Tencent ban on Claude Code, Codex or Cursor is documented as of November 2026. Tencent publishes strong adoption figures for its own CodeBuddy (over 90% of engineers; 50% of new code AI-assisted, October 2025; grade A). The only evidence on external tools is an employee account in Beijing Youth Daily that Claude Code was removed from the internal model pool in November 2025 and later restored, with most staff moving to CodeBuddy; Tencent declined to comment (grade B-/C).

### Cited Findings
- 24 October 2025, "2025 Tencent R&D Big Data Report": more than 90% of Tencent engineers use CodeBuddy, 50% of new code is AI-assisted, and monthly new code averages 325 million lines. Level for the in-house tool: encouraged. Grade A (self-reported, not independently verified). May be outdated. — [ifeng Tech](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8niMJjREUb3); [Yangtse Evening Post, 24 October 2025](https://www.yzwb.net/news/ch/202510/t20251024_280966.html)

## 4. Baidu, Huawei, Meituan, JD.com, Kuaishou, Xiaomi, NetEase, PDD, Didi and others

### Takeaway
JD.com and Meituan have the best-documented controls outside the big three: since spring 2026 both route external model use through approval, and both apply this to domestic rivals' models as well as foreign ones (grade B-). Kuaishou's restriction on Cursor rests on a netizen tip (grade C). For Baidu, Huawei and Xiaomi no rule on external tools was found, and for NetEase, PDD, Didi, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu the search could not be run. No policy should be inferred for those companies.

## 6. Supply-side barriers and how Chinese companies and engineers reacted

### Takeaway
Anthropic's September 2025 ownership rule is confirmed on its own site, and it led directly to Chinese-owned tools dropping Claude (Tencent's CodeBuddy international, then Trae on 4 November 2025). In 2026 the supply-side pressure grew: Anthropic ran a detection experiment in Claude Code against resellers and distillation and accused Alibaba of a distillation attack, which in turn triggered the Chinese-side bans of July 2026. Engineer workarounds (resellers, account bans, domestic substitutes) are documented only at anecdote level here.

## 7. Tool versus model: running Claude Code against domestic models

- Direction of travel from 2025 to November 2026: Alibaba went from "open" (2025) to subsidising (early 2026) to a vendor-specific ban (July 2026); ByteDance from a tool blacklist (2025) to subsidised trial (April 2026) to data-class control (November 2026); JD.com and Meituan to approval gateways (spring 2026); Tencent to steering by provisioning without a published ban.
- The answer to the wider question for China is "partly": several majors ban or gate specific external tools and models, but the documented rules are vendor-specific, data-class-specific or approval-based, and in-house tools often carry third-party models.
