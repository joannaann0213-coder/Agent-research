**Read this first: method and limits**

- All findings come from 28 web searches run on 26 Nov 2026. **No full article could be opened.** The page-fetch tool failed for every host tried (9to5google.com, the-decoder.com, www.techrepublic.com, www.thestar.com.my, www.htx.com, www.heise.de are outside this session's network allow-list). Every finding therefore rests on search-result summaries and headlines of the linked pages. Most links are secondary coverage of paywalled originals (Business Insider, Bloomberg, The Information, WSJ). Quotes are as rendered in those summaries and should be checked against the originals before publication.
- **The session's shared web-search budget ran out before the last planned batch ran.** Not searched as a result: the detail of The Information's 2026 Meta reports, Meta's approval history for Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and Gemini, Meta executive statements from 2026, MetaCode and Muse Code details, Google's Anthropic stake and Vertex AI, other Alphabet units, Apple enforcement, and the effect of the Apple and Google Gemini deal. - **Evidence grades.** A = on-record company statement or published memo text. B = reputable outlet citing internal documents or several employees. C = anecdote (X, Blind, single blogs, aggregator-only claims). "(2nd)" after a grade means the original was seen only through secondary coverage. Grade C items are leads, not established facts.
- **"May be outdated"** = not reconfirmed by anything dated after June 2026.
- **"Memory, unverified"** = background from the researcher's own knowledge, not checked in this session. Do not cite it without checking.
- **Tool versus model.** "Claude Code", "Codex", "Cursor" and "GitHub Copilot" below mean the tool or harness. "Claude", "GPT" and "Gemini" mean the model. The two are recorded separately throughout.

## 1. Google, the rule: what do Google's internal rules say about external AI coding tools on Google code?

### Takeaway
At the latest confirmation (Business Insider, 14 Sep 2026), standalone external coding tools such as Claude Code and Codex are still not opened for internal Google code, but every engineer can now select Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 as a model inside Google's internal Antigravity platform, on a per-user quota, with Gemini the stated primary model. So Google blocks the external harness for proprietary code and allows the external model inside its own tool; before September 2026 both were restricted to a few approved groups.

### Inferences
- The lead "2025 guidance required approval for third-party tools" is a simplification. The better-supported reading is: internal models only for coding, approval for third-party AI in non-coding work. The explicit exception route for Claude Code and Codex is first documented by Bloomberg in April 2026.
- From September 2026 the rule is about the harness, not the model: code stays inside a Google-controlled tool, and the model behind it may be Anthropic's. The stated reason for barring the external tools is security (Bloomberg).
- An open-source carve-out fits a rule designed to protect proprietary code, which is what Dogan's care to use only public concepts also suggests.
- "All engineers" in the September 2026 reports refers to access through Antigravity under quota. It does not mean free choice of tools.

### Gaps
- No primary text of the guidelines or of the Kacholia email was found, so the exact wording is unknown.
- Cursor and GitHub Copilot are not named in anything found. They would fall under "outside coding tools" by inference only.
- Whether the open-source carve-out for Claude Code still exists after September 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Quota size, the list of "select third-party models" beyond Claude Opus 5, and whether OpenAI models are included were not found.
- The exception process (who approves, how many exceptions) is undocumented beyond "show a work need".

## 2. Google, internal tools and leadership statements: which in-house tools, what share of code, and was use of internal agents required?

### Takeaway
Google engineers work mainly in Cider and in an internal build of Antigravity reported as "Jetski", on Gemini models, and Sundar Pichai put the AI-generated share of new code at 75% in April 2026 (up from 50% in autumn 2025 and about 25% in October 2024). The spring 2026 leads are confirmed at grade B: The Information reported a DeepMind "strike team" to close the coding gap with Anthropic and a Sergey Brin memo saying every Gemini engineer should use internal agents; whether that is a binding requirement is the reporters' characterization.
