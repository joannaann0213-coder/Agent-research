# Other tech companies and regions: how companies that do not sell a frontier AI coding assistant, and large tech companies outside the US and China, treat external AI coding tools (state as of 26 November 2026)

**Read this first: method and limits.** Research date 2026-11-26. I ran 49 web searches. The session's shared web-search budget then ran out, so 12 planned follow-up searches were not executed (Japanese-language searches for LINE Yahoo, CyberAgent, DeNA, SoftBank, Rakuten, NTT Data, Sony, Toyota, Hitachi; targeted searches for Klarna, Zalando, Booking.com, SAP, Lyft, DoorDash, Intuit, Adobe, PayPal, eBay; and a verification search on the June 2026 US order concerning Anthropic models). Direct page fetches were blocked by the network policy (four hosts tried, all failed), so **no article was read in full**. Every finding below rests on search-result summaries and headlines, cross-checked across several results where possible. Quotes are "as given in search summaries". "No evidence found" means "nothing surfaced in this limited search", not "no policy exists".

**Legend.** Restriction level: total ban / banned for proprietary or production code only / officially unsupported or discouraged / approval required / allowed under an enterprise agreement / encouraged or mandated. A spend cap is noted as a modifier. Evidence grade: A = on-record company statement or published memo text (A-vendor = vendor case study or vendor press release: confirms adoption, not the absence of restrictions); B = reputable outlet citing internal documents or several employees; C = anecdote, small blog or aggregator. **"May be outdated"** = I could not reconfirm it with a source dated June 2026 or later. "Harness" = the tool (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot); "model" = Claude, GPT, Gemini and so on.

## 1. US internet and software companies without a competing frontier coding model: which AI coding tools may or must engineers use?

### Takeaway
Every documented case in this group is adoption of external tools, usually several harnesses side by side, and at Shopify and Coinbase use is an explicit expectation or mandate; I found no ban on external AI coding tools at any of them. The limits that appeared in 2026 are spending caps (Uber in June, Tesla in July), and for about half of the named companies nothing was documented in this session.

Headline phrase: "Reflexive AI usage is now a baseline expectation at Shopify." AI-usage questions were added to performance and peer reviews; teams must show why AI cannot do the work before asking for headcount; "This applies to all of us, including me and the executive teams." The memo concerns AI use in general, not a named coding tool. Level: encouraged or mandated. Grade A. - Uber shows that an encouraged rollout can turn into a hard cap within about two months of the budget overrun, for cost reasons alone.

### Gaps
- **No evidence found** (one combined search each returned nothing relevant; follow-up searches were not run because the budget ran out): DoorDash, Lyft, Pinterest, Reddit, eBay, PayPal, Adobe, Intuit. **Not searched at all:** Datadog, Snowflake, Palantir. No policy should be inferred for any of them.

- Coinbase: nothing found for 2026, including whether the 50% target was met or whether the mandate still stands.
- Airbnb: the 60% figure rests on an aggregator; the Business Insider and TechCrunch originals were not opened.
- Netflix: no company-wide written policy or licence count found.
- I found no written rule at any of these companies that bans AI tools for production or proprietary code only.

## 2. Large technology companies with their own enterprise AI products but no leading coding model: who mandates an in-house assistant, who standardises on external tools, and are any external tools banned?

### Takeaway
No ban on external tools is documented at any company in this group. The two best-documented cases, Nvidia (Cursor for all engineers) and Salesforce (Cursor plus the in-house CodeGenie as default agents, despite selling Agentforce Vibes), are standardisation on an external harness; IBM is the nearest thing to "in-house first" (more than 80,000 employees on its own Bob), but Bob itself runs partly on Anthropic's Claude.

Level: encouraged or mandated (external harness). Grade A (CEO on record). ### Inferences
- Owning an enterprise AI or coding product does not predict a ban on external tools in this group. Salesforce and ServiceNow sell developer agents and still roll out Cursor and Claude Code to their own engineers.
- The harness and the model must be separated to read these cases: IBM promotes its own harness but buys an external model; Salesforce and Nvidia buy an external harness.

### Gaps
- **No evidence found** on internal policies at Oracle, SAP, Intel, Dell or Workday. No policy should be inferred.
- The Nvidia claim is from late 2025; I found no 2026 reconfirmation. One search summary mentioned a headline about OpenAI Codex being deployed to 30,000 Nvidia engineers; I could not verify it and do not treat it as a finding.
- IBM: whether Bob is mandatory, and whether external harnesses are blocked on IBM devices, is undocumented.
- Salesforce: the 75% versus 90% discrepancy is unresolved; the dates of Benioff's remarks are unconfirmed.

## 4. Reversals and limits in 2026: who capped, cut or restricted external tools, who went from ban to permission, and why

### Takeaway
The dated 2026 reversals in this comparison group run in two directions: cost caps at heavy adopters (Uber in June, Tesla in July, Walmart later in the year) and the lifting of security-driven bans at Korean manufacturers (Samsung in May to June, SK hynix under review from June). I found no in-scope company that restricted AI coding tools because of an outage blamed on AI-generated code.

### Cited Findings

- Whether any European company moved its developers off Claude Code after June 2026 is not documented in what I found.

## 9. Pattern: what explains the differences between companies?

### Takeaway
In this comparison group, owning a competing product does not predict restriction: several companies that sell coding agents or own models (Salesforce, Atlassian, ServiceNow, LG, Samsung, Rakuten, Block) run external tools at scale. The documented restrictions are explained by security posture in IP-sensitive hardware businesses (Samsung until mid-2026, SK hynix) and by token cost (Uber, Tesla, Walmart); the hypothesis gets partial support only from Tesla's xAI carve-out and IBM's in-house-first rollout.

### Cited Findings
- **Evidence against the hypothesis (owners of competing products or models that adopt external tools).** Salesforce sells Agentforce Vibes and made Cursor a default agent ([Salesforce Engineering](https://engineering.salesforce.com/building-sustainable-enterprise-ai-adoption-cultural-strategies-that-achieved-95-developer-engagement/)). ServiceNow rolled out Claude Code company-wide ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/servicenow-anthropic-claude)). Atlassian's Rovo Dev coexists with Cursor ([project page](https://haihuang.ai/selected-work/rovo-dev-cli/), grade C). LG owns EXAONE and signed a group-wide Claude Enterprise deal with Claude Code ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10767160)). Samsung owns Gauss and bought ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/samsung-electronics-chatgpt-codex-deployment/)). Naver told 4,500 staff to use Cursor ([Nate News](https://news.nate.com/view/20250615n05106)). IBM's own Bob runs partly on Claude ([DevOps.com](https://devops.com/ibm-moves-its-bob-coding-agent-inside-the-firewall)).
- **Evidence against the hypothesis (companies with no competing product that still restrict).** Uber caps agentic coding tools at $1,500 per tool per month ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-02/uber-caps-usage-of-ai-tools-like-claude-code-to-cut-costs)). SK hynix restricts general external AI agents on security grounds ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10770334)). Kakao blocks tools that have not passed security review ([Asia Business Daily](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025070406431469035)). Samsung's 2023 to 2026 ban was triggered by leaks, not by a rival product ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/11/09/samsung_introduces_generative_ai_on_devices/)).
- **Evidence for the hypothesis.** Tesla's cap on third-party AI tools exempts xAI products ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spending-200-week/)). IBM reports 80,000+ employees on its own Bob and no external-harness rollout is documented ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/10/02/ibm-brings-its-bob-ai-coding-agent-inside-firewalls-for-regulated-companies/)). Companies with no competing product and no IP-leak history adopt freely or mandate: Shopify ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/04/08/internal-memo-shopify-ceo-declares-ai-non-optional/)), Coinbase ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/22/coinbase-ceo-explains-why-he-fired-engineers-who-didnt-try-ai-immediately)), Nvidia ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/10/15/jensen-huang-says-every-nvidia-engineer-now-codes-with-cursor)), Spotify ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/12/spotify-says-its-best-developers-havent-written-a-line-of-code-since-december-thanks-to-ai/)).
- **Cost as a driver.** Uber, Tesla and Walmart all introduced caps in June to autumn 2026 after usage outran budgets ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-02/uber-caps-usage-of-ai-tools-like-claude-code-to-cut-costs); [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spending-200-week/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/walmart-says-token-limit-employee-085002872.html)). Shopify is the documented counter-case with no cap ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/how-shopify-built-an-ai-stack-that-doesnt-care-which-models-survive)).
- **Supply and sovereignty as a driver.** European executives responded to US access limits by diversifying providers, not banning US tools ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/analysisus-curbs-on-ai-spur-european-firms-to-spread-the-risk-4700367)).

### Inferences
- Ranking of explanations by how many documented cases each fits: (1) security posture and IP sensitivity (Samsung, SK hynix, Kakao's review gate, the Korean cloud-versus-closed-network debate); (2) cost under token pricing (Uber, Tesla, Walmart); (3) regulated clients and data residency (Capgemini's on-premises Mistral Code, IBM's air-gapped Bob as a product); (4) owning or being tied to a competing product (Tesla and xAI; weakly IBM). Factor 4 fits the fewest cases in this group.
- The form of restriction differs by cause: security concerns produced bans and approval gates; cost produced caps that keep the tool available; affiliation produced an exemption for the related product.
- "In-house tool" usually means an in-house harness or gateway on external models (IBM Bob, Stripe Minions, Spotify Honk, Shopify's proxy, Walmart Code Puppy). Counting these as "in-house only" would overstate how many companies avoid external AI.
- This comparison group cannot, by itself, confirm or refute the hypothesis for the companies that do sell frontier coding assistants; it only shows that outside that group bans are rare and that security and cost explain most restrictions.

### Gaps
- The sample is biased toward companies that publicise adoption (earnings calls, vendor case studies). Companies with restrictive policies have less reason to announce them, so the absence of documented bans understates restriction.
- About 30 named companies have no evidence at all in this session (see the last table rows).
- No article was opened in full; grades reflect the kind of source, not a verified reading of it.
