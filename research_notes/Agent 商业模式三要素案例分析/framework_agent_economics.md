## Q1. Standard formulations of the three elements (English and Chinese), how they map, and what "商业模式三要素" usually means

### Takeaway
The English canon agrees on the arc "create/deliver value → capture value." Teece (2010) calls the business model the "architecture of the value creation, delivery, and capture mechanisms." Johnson, Christensen and Kagermann (2008) use four boxes: customer value proposition, profit formula, key resources and key processes. Osterwalder splits the same ground into nine blocks. Chinese discourse has no single authoritative "三要素" definition. Its most common triad is 价值主张/价值定义 → 价值创造与传递 → 价值获取 (the "价值三角形"), or the shorter 创造价值 → 传递价值 → 获取价值. The well-known 魏朱 model has six elements, not three. The report should therefore state its own definition and give an explicit mapping.

### Cited Findings
**English canon**
- [Primary/Background, 2010] Teece, "Business Models, Business Strategy and Innovation," *Long Range Planning* 43(2–3): 172–194. The article describes a business model as the way an enterprise "creates and delivers value to customers, and then converts payments received to profits." On p.191 it calls the business model the "architecture of the value creation, delivery, and capture mechanisms." Teece also notes that the concept lacks established theoretical grounding in economics or business studies. — [Teece 2010 (KTH-hosted PDF)](https://www.kth.se/social/upload/528c4881f2765458b97a9368/Teece%20%282010%29.pdf). ScienceDirect returned 403, so this relies on the hosted PDF as summarized in search.
- [Primary/Background, Dec 2008] Johnson, Christensen & Kagermann, "Reinventing Your Business Model," *HBR* (Dec 2008) — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/12/reinventing-your-business-model). The model has four interlocking elements:
  - **Customer value proposition (CVP):** do an important "job" for a target customer better than the alternatives.
  - **Profit formula:** revenue model (price × volume), cost structure, margin model, and resource velocity (the assets, fixed costs, margins and velocity needed to cover them).
  - **Key resources:** people, technology, products, facilities, equipment, funding, brand.
  - **Key processes:** how the CVP is delivered in a repeatable, scalable way.
  - Sources: [Business Process Incubator summary](https://www.businessprocessincubator.com/?p=4039); [Flevy summary](https://flevy.com/browse/marketplace/business-model-innovation-136/slide-framework-defining-customer-value-profitability). These are secondary summaries; the HBR page shows only a teaser.
- [Background, 2005/2010] Osterwalder & Pigneur's Business Model Canvas, popularized in *Business Model Generation* (2010), has nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure. A common criticism is that it is static and says little about how the blocks interact. — [Wikipedia: Business Model Canvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Model_Canvas). Practitioners describe it as a one-page view of how a firm "creates, delivers, and captures value" — [Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/business-model-canvas-0).

**Chinese formulations**
- [Primary/Background, 2010] 张敬伟、王迎军, 《基于价值三角形逻辑的商业模式概念模型研究》, 《外国经济与管理》 2010, 32(6): 1–8. The paper builds a three-dimensional "价值三角形" model of **价值定义 – 价值创造与传递 – 价值获取** and applies "5W1H" to business-model design. — [上海财经大学期刊页](https://qks.shufe.edu.cn/J/ArticleQuery/A160824002159/CN)
- [Practitioner] Many Chinese explainers treat the model as a closed loop of **创造价值 → 传递价值 → 获取价值**. Creating value starts from customer needs, delivery relies on resource allocation and activities, and capture relies on the 盈利模式. — [前瞻问答](https://www.qianzhan.com/wenda/detail/191028-115291b5.html)
- [Teaching material] A Shanghai university courseware PDF uses the progression **价值发现 → 价值匹配 (partners) → 价值获取**. — [华东师大课件 PDF](https://sie.ecnu.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/de/08/c04c5d7d444a9d0f3921bd108599/afb598e7-4944-483a-9188-5a534d2c14ca.pdf)
- [Academic/practitioner] The **魏朱 (魏炜、朱武祥) six-element model** consists of 定位, 业务系统, 关键资源能力, 盈利模式, 自由现金流结构 and 企业价值.
  - It defines the business model as **"利益相关者的交易结构."**
  - 业务系统 is treated as the core.
  - 企业价值 (the discounted value of future free cash flow) is the goal and result.
  - Sources: [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/pmd/963953.html); [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/1721310789633). These are secondary explainers; the original book was not checked.
- [Source scan] A search across Chinese sources found no authoritative, unified definition of "商业模式三要素." The three-part create/deliver/capture loop is most common. In the Canvas, 价值主张 is one of nine blocks. — [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/share/6054677.html); [前瞻问答](https://www.qianzhan.com/wenda/detail/191028-115291b5.html)

### Inferences
- **Mapping (my synthesis):**

| Report's element | Teece 2010 | Johnson–Christensen–Kagermann 2008 | Osterwalder Canvas | 张敬伟–王迎军 2010 | 魏朱 six-element |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① 价值主张 (for whom, which job, why better) | "value proposition for the customer" | Customer value proposition (job to be done) | Customer segments + Value proposition (Channels and Relationships sit partly here) | 价值定义 | 定位 |
| ② 价值创造与传递 (resources, processes, partners, channels, cost drivers) | value "creation and delivery mechanisms" | Key resources + Key processes | Key resources, Key activities, Key partners, Channels, Customer relationships (+ Cost structure) | 价值创造与传递 | 业务系统 + 关键资源能力 |
| ③ 价值获取 (payer, revenue model, pricing unit, margins, defensibility) | "capture mechanisms" / converting payments to profits | Profit formula (revenue model, cost structure, margin model, resource velocity) | Revenue streams + Cost structure | 价值获取 | 盈利模式 + 自由现金流结构 + 企业价值 |

- When Chinese business media say "三要素," they most often mean the create/deliver/capture triad, sometimes with 价值主张 in place of 创造价值 as the first step. The report's formulation (价值主张 / 价值创造与传递 / 价值获取) is the 张–王 "价值三角形" with 价值定义 renamed 价值主张. It maps cleanly onto Teece and onto the four JCK boxes (JCK's two delivery boxes merge into element ②).
- **Cost structure belongs to both ② and ③**, depending on the framework. JCK puts it inside the profit formula (capture), while the Canvas treats it as its own block. For agents, a clean convention is:
  - Inference/compute cost is a creation-side driver, tested in ②.
  - Gross margin and unit economics are the capture-side test, in ③.
- **For agents, "传递" (delivery) takes on extra meaning.** It covers the channel where the agent lives (IM, Slack/Teams, IDE, CRM, super-app) and the integrations and permissions that let it act. These map to the Canvas's Channels and Key Partners and to 魏朱's 业务系统 (the transaction structure with platform owners).

### Gaps
- Richardson (2008), "The business model: an integrative framework for strategy execution" (*Strategic Change*), is commonly credited with the exact triad "value proposition / value creation and delivery system / value capture." I could not retrieve the paper, so this attribution is unverified.
- No quantitative evidence (such as frequency counts) shows which formulation dominates Chinese discourse; the judgment above is qualitative.
- 原磊 (2007) "3-4-8" model and other Chinese academic variants were not checked.

## Q2. How analysts, VCs and operators adapt the framework to AI agents

### Takeaway
Investors recast the agent value proposition from "a tool for a worker" to "the work itself": services-as-software (Foundation Capital), autopilot versus copilot (Sequoia), and "software is eating labor" (a16z). That makes the payer's labor or outsourcing budget the relevant pool, not the IT budget. It pushes delivery toward embedded, services-heavy models (forward-deployed engineers) and capture toward usage or outcome pricing. Spend data through December 2025, however, still show copilots dominating and few true agents in production. Incumbents have also adapted by renaming bundles and metrics as "digital labor" (Salesforce's Agentic Work Units, Microsoft's E7 with Agent 365).

### Cited Findings
- **Foundation Capital**
  - [Background, Apr & Oct 2024] Joanne Chen and Jaya Gupta introduced "service-as-software" in April 2024 and followed with "Systems of Agents" in October 2024. Their argument is that software becomes the worker rather than a tool, replacing both software and labor spend. — [Marketing AI Institute summary](https://marketingaiinstitute.com/blog/service-as-software-ai)
  - [Opinion, publication date not shown] Ashu Garg and Jaya Gupta, "The $4.6T Services-as-Software opportunity: Lessons from the first year," frames the prize as the **$4.6T** enterprises spend on salaries and services, against a **$200B** SaaS market. — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/?p=12876)
- **Sequoia** [Opinion, 5 Mar 2026] Julien Bek, "Services: The New Software" — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/):
  - "A copilot sells the tool. An autopilot sells the work."
  - "If you sell the tool, you're in a race against the model."
  - "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services." Example: about $10K a year for QuickBooks versus about $120K for an accountant.
  - "Intelligence" work (coding, medical billing, NDA drafting) gets automated before "judgment" work.
  - "The outsourced task is the wedge. The insourced work is the long-term TAM." Replacing an outsourcing contract "is a vendor swap. Replacing headcount is a reorg."
  - Autopilots compound proprietary data about good judgment. Copilot vendors face an innovator's dilemma.
  - Software engineering is over half of AI tool usage.
  - Labor TAM examples: insurance brokerage $140–200B; IT managed services $100B+; management consulting $300–400B.
- **a16z** [Opinion, 3 Oct 2025] Alex Rampell's "Software is Eating Labor" (2025 LP Summit talk) contrasts a ~$300B SaaS market with the ~$13T US labor market and argues for the move from seat-based to outcome-based pricing. — [a16z podcast page](https://a16z.com/podcast/software-is-eating-labor/). Figures come from the episode description and summaries, not a transcript.
- **Bessemer** [Opinion + data, 13 Aug 2025] — [BVP State of AI 2025](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025):
  - "from systems of record to systems of action."
  - "For AI application founders, context and memory may be the new moats," with switching costs that "may become almost emotional."
  - Vertical AI is "the new SaaS."
  - For agentic products, "security, autonomy, and reliability remain unsolved."
  - Labs "rolling out agents" are accelerating bundling, and "Supernovas" face "thin wrapper" labels.
- **ICONIQ** [Survey, Jan 2026 bi-annual snapshot, ~300 software executives] — [SaaStr summary](https://www.saastr.com/iconiqs-latest-state-of-ai-report-the-10-most-important-data-points-for-saas-founders):
  - Median share of customers receiving forward-deployed-engineer (FDE) support: 20% (2024), 27% (2025), 32% (2026 projected).
  - 44% of companies use FDEs in a hybrid product/delivery role.
  - 40% of companies with $500M+ revenue actively deploy customer-facing agents, versus 28% of sub-$100M companies.
  - "Few" companies have data ready for AI agents.
- **Menlo Ventures** [Survey + model, 9 Dec 2025] — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/):
  - Horizontal-AI spend was 86% copilots ($7.2B), 10% agent platforms ($750M) and 5% personal productivity ($450M).
  - Only 16% of enterprise deployments and 27% of startup deployments qualify as true agents (plan, act, observe, adapt).
- **Sierra** [Opinion/Reported] Bret Taylor frames Sierra as outcome-priced "productized BPO." — [AgentScout (secondary)](https://agentscout.pages.dev/biz/startups/review/20260508-sierra-business-model-deep-dive-bret-taylor-ai-agent-empire/)
- **Incumbents use "digital labor" framing in official metrics**
  - [Primary, 15 May 2025] Salesforce's pricing release was titled "…to Accelerate the Digital Labor Revolution." — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250515332990/en/Salesforce-Introduces-New-Flexible-Agentforce-Pricing-to-Accelerate-the-Digital-Labor-Revolution)
  - [Primary, 26 Aug 2026] Salesforce reports **Agentic Work Units**: 7.0B delivered to date across Agentforce and Slack, including 3.2B in Q2 FY27 (+97% QoQ). From Q2 FY27, Agentforce ARR "includes our AI offerings, Slackbot and Headless 360." — [Salesforce Q2 FY27 release](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Delivers-Record-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx)
  - [Reported, GA 1 May 2026] Microsoft 365 E7 "Frontier Suite" costs $99/user/month and bundles E5, M365 Copilot, Entra Suite and **Agent 365** (an agent management and governance layer). — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/microsoft-copilot/new-microsoft-365-e7-subscription-bundles-copilot-and-agent-365); [Office Watch](https://office-watch.com/2026/microsoft-365-e7-a-99-frontier-suite-the-future-or-an-expensive-bundle/). Agent compute is billed separately through Copilot Studio/Foundry — [TrustedTech](https://www.trustedtechteam.com/blogs/microsoft-365/how-much-is-microsoft-365-e7-pricing-2026)
  - [Primary, 15 Jun 2026] Fin's agent "resolves complex customer queries end-to-end, across every channel," including live chat, email, WhatsApp, SMS, phone and Slack. — [Salesforce press release on Fin](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Fin/). Other researchers cover agents inside IM/collaboration channels (Claude in Slack, @grok on X, Tencent WorkBuddy); they are mentioned here only in passing.

### Inferences
- **Applied to the three elements, the VC reframing changes each test:**
  - ① The "job" becomes a unit of finished work, and the alternative becomes human labor or BPO, not other software.
  - ② Delivery needs access to systems of record, presence in the user's channel, and permission to act, often with services attached (FDEs).
  - ③ The payer becomes a line-of-business owner with a labor or outsourcing budget, and pricing units shift toward actions, resolutions or tasks.
- **Thesis and spend diverge.** Menlo's 2025 split (86% copilots versus 10% agent platforms; only 16% of enterprise deployments are true agents) suggests most value captured so far is still tool-like, priced per seat or per unit of consumption, rather than "selling work."
- **The FDE trend re-imports services economics.** Delivery cost and gross margin of "services-as-software" sit between SaaS and services. This is consistent with the AI margin data in Q4.
- **Systems-of-record and channel owners are adapting fastest.** Salesforce and Microsoft rename seat bundles and usage metrics as "digital labor" (AWUs, Agent 365) and count channel bots (Slackbot) in agent ARR. That makes them both the delivery channel and the capture point.

### Gaps
- I found no primary sources on "multiplayer" or "ambient" agent framings (for example LangChain's "ambient agents") in this pass.
- Battery, Emergence, a Bessemer 2026 "State of AI" and an a16z 2026 enterprise CIO survey were not retrieved.
- The publication date of Foundation Capital's "$4.6T…Lessons from the first year" piece is not shown on the fetched page.

## Q3. Pricing and revenue models agent products use in 2025–2026, with concrete prices, and evidence on which work

### Takeaway
Hybrid pricing is now the norm.
- **Seats remain the anchor:** M365 Copilot at $30/user/month; M365 E7 bundle at $99.
- **Meters are spreading fast:** actions or credits (Agentforce Flex Credits at $0.10 per action; GitHub AI Credits at $0.01 each, token-metered from 1 Jun 2026), compute units (Devin ACUs) and rate limits (Claude Code weekly caps).
- **Outcome pricing is established in customer service:** Fin at $0.99 per outcome; Zendesk at about $1.50–2.00 per automated resolution; Sierra.

The strongest revenue evidence is at bundling incumbents (30M+ paid M365 Copilot seats; Agentforce above $1.5B ARR under a broadened definition) and at usage-metered coding agents (Cursor above $4B run-rate by June 2026). Outcome-priced customer-service leaders reached roughly $100–200M ARR. Many of these companies were then consolidated by larger platforms: Manus by Meta, Fin by Salesforce, Cursor by SpaceX/xAI.

## Q4. AI-application gross margins and inference costs versus classic SaaS, and how they constrain value capture

### Takeaway
AI-application gross margins run about 25–60%, against roughly 70–80%+ for classic SaaS. ICONIQ's survey shows them improving (41% → 45% → 52% projected for 2026), with inference at about 23% of revenue. Per-token prices keep falling, but agents use 5–30× more tokens per task and frontier models cost more per token, so cost per task can rise. Gartner forecasts inference cost per agentic workflow up more than 5× through 2028. In 2026, China's token price war also reversed into price increases amid compute scarcity. Value capture for agents therefore depends on three things:
- pricing units that pass variable cost through to the customer;
- model routing or owning a model;
- an enterprise-heavy customer mix.

Flat-priced consumer and prosumer plans are the weakest position.

### Inferences
- **Agent unit economics (my framing):** gross profit per task = price per unit of work − (tokens per task × price per token + tool and infrastructure cost + human-in-the-loop or FDE cost). Tokens per task rise with autonomy (5–30×) and with frontier-model pricing, so cheaper tokens do not automatically widen margins. Capture holds only if the price is indexed to usage or outcome, or if the company cuts cost per task through routing or its own models (Fin's Apex, Cursor's Composer).
- **Margin follows customer segment, model ownership and pricing unit:**
  - Enterprise beats prosumer (Cursor; MiniMax's B-end 69.4% versus C-end 4.7%).
  - Owning a model beats renting from a competitor (Cursor/Anthropic).
  - Metered pricing beats flat pricing.
- **The ~60% "Shooting Star" margin is a practical bar** for "value capture holds" (Bessemer). ICONIQ's 52% average for 2026 means the median AI product is still below it.
- **Incumbents can absorb AI cost inside bundles** while keeping blended margins around 81% (Salesforce). That pressures standalone agent prices and favors bundled distribution.
- **Compute scarcity in 2026 adds supply risk** to both elements ② and ③: China's price increases, Kimi's subscription pause, and compute access cited as Cursor's constraint.

- The original Gartner research note was not accessed. The Epoch and Goldman numbers are second-hand.
- No rigorous cross-company measurement of tokens per task exists.
- The ICONIQ "July 2026" edition is unverified.

## Q5. Preconditions sources identify for each element to hold for agents

### Takeaway
Pulling the sources together:
- **Element ① (value proposition)** holds when the task is frequent, rule-bound ("intelligence" rather than "judgment"), and has a verifiable outcome, with a clear external alternative to displace (outsourced or BPO spend).
- **Element ② (creation and delivery)** holds when the agent has privileged access to context and data, systems of record and the user's channel, plus reliability and governance, and a compute supply at a viable cost.
- **Element ③ (value capture)** holds when a payer can move labor or outsourcing budget to the agent, the pricing unit tracks both value and variable cost, and the firm can defend itself against model providers and channel owners.

The 2025–26 consolidation wave (Manus to Meta, Fin to Salesforce, Cursor to SpaceX/xAI) suggests the last condition is the hardest for independents.

### Inferences
- **A test rubric for whether an agent business model "holds up" (成立) (my synthesis):**

| Element | Must-pass tests | Evidence that it passes | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① 价值主张 | Named payer and user; frequent "intelligence" task; outcome verifiable; clear alternative cost (human, BPO or SaaS) | Measured resolution or completion rate (e.g., Fin's 76%); displaces an outsourced line item; daily use (coding: 50% of devs daily) | Pilots with no EBIT link (McKinsey's 39%); "agent-washing"; outputs that still need judgment-heavy review |
| ② 价值创造与传递 | Access to context and data (system of record, memory); presence in the channel where work happens; permissions and integrations to act; reliability and governance; model supply and cost per task | Proprietary or fine-tuned model; multi-model routing; embedded in IM/CRM/IDE; FDE-backed onboarding that becomes productized | Single competing lab as sole supplier; no token caps; compute shortages; a channel owned by a rival |
| ③ 价值获取 | Payer moves labor or outsourcing budget; pricing unit tracks value and variable cost; gross margin heading toward ~60%; defensible against labs and channel/SoR owners | Usage or outcome meters (actions, resolutions, credits); enterprise mix; data or judgment flywheel; ownership of the customer relationship | Flat unlimited plans on frontier models; consumer-only mix (MiniMax C-end 4.7% gross margin); value captured by the bundler (E7, Agentforce) or acquirer |

- **The three elements interact.** A strong ① (verifiable outcome) is what makes outcome pricing possible in ③. A weak ② (no own model, a rented channel) caps ③ through margin and through bargaining power held by the channel or model owner.

### Gaps
- Few sources quantify thresholds, for example the minimum resolution rate at which per-resolution pricing is profitable, or human-handled cost per contact for comparison.
- Trust and governance metrics (incident rates, error costs) for agents were not found.
- A newer McKinsey 2026 survey exists (its 2025 page links to "latest survey results"), but its agent figures could not be verified; secondary claims of "40% of $1B+ firms scaling agents" are unverified.

### Inferences
- **① in China:** usage-led value propositions are strong but willingness to pay is concentrated in heavy users (DAU paid rate 18.5% versus MAU 0.9%). Paid tiers aimed at power users (coding plans, pro tiers) are more viable than mass subscriptions, as Doubao's backlash and Kimi's tier fights show.
- **② in China:** distribution belongs to ecosystem owners (ByteDance, Alibaba). Independent agent companies tend to win by going overseas (Manus, Kimi's overseas growth) or by selling API and enterprise services.
- **③ in China, four observed paths:**
  - (a) API and token sales to developers and enterprises (Volcano Engine, MiniMax, Kimi);
  - (b) commerce or transaction monetization inside ecosystems (Qwen with Taobao and Alipay; the take-rate mechanics are not disclosed);
  - (c) project-based government and enterprise private deployment (procurement data);
  - (d) consumer subscriptions, which carry backlash risk. Ad-based monetization of agents was not evidenced in this pass.
- **The 2026 price reversal squeezes independent agent apps** that run on rented tokens and cannot charge consumers. It favors vertically integrated platforms that monetize indirectly. This is the opposite of the 2024 "price war" narrative and should be stated explicitly with dates in the report.
