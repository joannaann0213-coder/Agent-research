## Q0. Baseline economics (latest = Q3 2026, reported 2026-10-29) and the levers an agent could move

### Takeaway
Q3 2026 (reported 2026-10-29): about 993M orders, $33.9B Marketplace GOV and a 13.6% net revenue margin. That works out to about $34 GOV, $4.65 revenue, $1.79 Contribution Profit and $1.07 Adj. EBITDA per order. Ads are what now holds take rate up while consumer fees fall. Dasher payout is deducted inside revenue, so any fulfillment cost saving goes straight into take rate. The same day, DoorDash agreed to buy Instacart for about $17B.

### Inferences
- Lever map. Which scenario moves which number:
  - S1 (in-app agent): frequency, conversion, AOV and new-merchant trial. These feed GOV × take rate and ad inventory.
  - S2 (third-party agents): top-of-funnel acquisition (S&M was 2.3% of GOV, about $765M in Q3 2026), but also the risk of losing ad surface.
  - S3 (merchant agents): ads spend (Smart Campaigns), Commerce Platform and SaaS revenue, and merchant onboarding speed and selection.
  - S4 (fulfillment): Dasher payout, which is netted inside revenue and is the biggest cost line.
  - S5 (internal efficiency): R&D, G&A and support, which are "Contribution Profit"-level costs.
- Illustrative sensitivities at Q3 2026 scale [DERIVED]:
  - +1% orders ≈ +9.9M orders ≈ +$46M revenue a quarter at the average $4.65 revenue per order.
  - -$0.10 Dasher cost per order ≈ +$99M quarterly revenue ≈ +0.29pt of net revenue margin.
  - So per unit, fulfillment-side automation (S4) has the most direct P&L leverage. Consumer agents (S1) work through volume and AOV.
- Ads are now the swing factor that stabilizes take rate while DashPass pushes consumer fees down. Any agent channel that moves discovery off DoorDash's own surfaces (S2) puts pressure on exactly this line.

### Gaps
- DoorDash hasn't published an ads revenue or run-rate figure for 2025 or 2026; ads aren't broken out in SEC filings.
- No member count since end-2025 (combined >35M), and no separate US DashPass count.
- No disclosed AOV, Dasher cost per order, or segment-level contribution profit (US vs international, restaurant vs grocery).
- No data on the economics of Drive or Storefront/digital ordering beyond growth rates.

## Q1 (S1). Consumer ordering/decision agent inside DoorDash's own channels (Ask DoorDash, Text DoorDash, Zesty)

### Takeaway
In 2026 DoorDash shipped a stack of first-party agents: Ask DoorDash in the app (2026-06-11), a developer CLI (July), and Text DoorDash by text message plus an MCP connector (2026-09-30). It also tested Zesty, a standalone AI discovery app (December 2025). Company-reported results are strong: carts built 5x faster, nearly 50% higher grocery basket value, and almost half of Ask restaurant orders going to restaurants the customer hadn't tried. None of these products is monetized directly; value is captured through more orders, bigger baskets and more ad inventory.

## Q7. Risks: competition, regulation, data/privacy, inference cost, merchant pushback, deal risk

### Takeaway
The main agent-era risks:
- Discovery moving to third-party assistants, which DoorDash itself flags in its 10-Q risk factors.
- Rivals shipping equivalent AI (Uber's cart assistant, Instacart's grocer AI, Gemini automating Uber Eats and Grubhub as easily as DoorDash).
- Regulatory cost floors on Dashers.
- EU AI Act, GDPR and consumer-health-data limits on personalization.
- Capital intensity: replatform, autonomy, and the Instacart deal with its antitrust review into 2027–28.

Inference cost is managed but undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Competition risk from AI: "Our competitors or other third parties may incorporate AI into their products more quickly or more successfully than us". AI agents "may alter how consumers discover, access, and engage" and could "increase visibility for competing offerings". AI outputs that are "deficient, inaccurate, inappropriate, or biased" create liability, and "data leakage" is also a risk — [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000070/dash-20260930.htm)
- [OFFICIAL] On data and privacy, DoorDash is subject to GDPR and the EU "Artificial Intelligence Act". It notes that "new and evolving requirements and risks around consumer health data may restrict our ability to provide personalized content on our platform" — [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000070/dash-20260930.htm)
- [PRESS, 2026-06-11] Uber launched an AI cart assistant in 2026 that builds grocery lists from photos and prompts. Instacart launched AI tools for grocers in late 2025 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/doordash-ai-ordering-automation.html)
- [PRESS, 2026-02-25] Gemini's task automation supports Uber Eats, Grubhub and Instacart alongside DoorDash, so a general agent can switch providers — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/gemini-can-now-automate-some-multi-step-tasks-on-android)
- [PRESS, 2026-03-27] Instacart says only a very small percentage of its orders come directly through AI agents. It treats AI interfaces as a discovery channel, not a place to transact — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/what-went-wrong-with-chatgpts-instant-checkout/)
- [OFFICIAL] Autonomy competitors "may develop and commercialize autonomous and drone delivery technologies at scale before we or our partners do". Autonomy rules are "nascent and rapidly evolving" — [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000070/dash-20260930.htm)
- [OFFICIAL] Labor regulation: minimum earnings standards have forced fee increases on consumers and merchants. There are ongoing investigations into Dasher classification and pay practices (for example a CA EDD assessment, January 2023). Prop 22 is cited as bringing "increasing legislative and regulatory certainty" — [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000070/dash-20260930.htm). NYC's pay floor is $22.13/hr from 2026-04-01 — [NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/009-26/major-victory-nyc-)
- [OFFICIAL, 2026-10-29] The Instacart deal requires HSR, Canada and Israel approvals and an Instacart shareholder vote. It is funded largely with debt (a bridge facility), DoorDash targets keeping investment-grade ratings, and close is not expected before end-2027 or H1 2028. DoorDash commits to "No Grocery Exclusivity" — [Instacart deal PR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000114036126041111/ef20084547_ex99-1.htm)
- [PRESS] The market has pushed back on DoorDash's investment intensity: its worst day on record after the Nov 2025 guide for "several hundred million dollars" of 2026 investment, and the stock down 33% YTD at 2026-06-11 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/doordash-ai-ordering-automation.html)
- [OFFICIAL] On inference cost, DoorDash uses an internal token router to "apply the right amount of cost to the intelligence required" — [Q2 2026 PR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000048/proddashex991-pressrelease.htm). Q3 2026 R&D growth was attributed partly to "third-party software expenses" — [Q3 2026 PR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000062/proddashex991-pressrelease.htm)
- [OFFICIAL] On merchant fees and affordability, consumer fees as % of GOV are falling, with DashPass giving -9% transaction fees per order over two years. Ads and lower promotions make up for it — [Q3 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000070/dash-20260930.htm); [Q3 2026 PR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000062/proddashex991-pressrelease.htm). Marketplace commission tiers remain 15/25/30% — [merchant pricing](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/products/pricing)

### Inferences
- Disintermediation risk is real in principle but small in practice so far (several hundred orders). DoorDash's defense has three parts:
  - keep checkout, account and DashPass in-house in every integration;
  - ship its own off-app agents (Text DoorDash, MCP) so "agentic" demand still runs through DoorDash;
  - make fulfillment quality ("atoms") the moat.
- The specific value at risk is ads, which now stabilize take rate. If agents commoditize providers (as Gemini can with DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub), sponsored placement inside DoorDash loses reach unless DoorDash can sell placement inside agents, an idea Xu floated in Oct 2026.
- Merchant pushback on fees is likely to focus on commissions (15–30%) and ad "tax". The commission-free AI websites and marketer are partly a response: they give merchants a cheaper own channel while keeping them in DoorDash's ecosystem through Drive and DashOS.

### Gaps
- No disclosed AI spend, so inference-cost risk can't be quantified.
- No found 2025–2026 data on merchant fee disputes or caps specific to DoorDash beyond the 10-Q language (for example new city fee caps).
- Antitrust commentary on the Instacart deal (FTC posture, analyst views) wasn't researched in this pass.
- Amazon's 2026 food or restaurant-delivery moves relative to DoorDash weren't researched beyond TechCrunch's note that Amazon blocks personal agents.
